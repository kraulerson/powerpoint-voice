## Framing correction that applies to BUG-66 and BUG-67

The report leans hard on *"the project already knew it — `docs/design-notes/voice-engine-design.md:46`"* and *"`:58` requires `validateGrammarJson()` to reject a missing `[unk]`. Neither shipped."* Both line citations are accurate. The framing is not: that document's own header reads **"Status: DESIGNED, NOT IMPLEMENTED. Deferred by Orchestrator decision 2026-08-04… All three critics returned NEEDS_CHANGES; their findings MUST be folded in before implementation."** It specifies a different module — `src/voice/voice_grammar.hpp`, `commandGrammarPhrases()`, `buildGrammarJson()` — and **`src/voice/` does not exist**. So `[unk]` is not an unmet requirement of shipped F8c/F8d; it is a deferred proposal whose warning happens to be correct. That weakens "the project knew and shipped anyway" to "the knowledge existed in the repo and was not carried into the module that shipped." The technical claim stands regardless — I reproduced it from audio, not from the document.

## Also verified (negative controls hold)

2 min each of digital silence, 440 Hz tone, loud white noise (σ=9000), and 4 Hz amplitude-modulated speech-shaped babble → **0 finals**. Low-level white noise (σ=300) → 6× `"hundred"`, **0 commands**. `modelIsGrammarCapable` static-graph refusal and the BUG-65 unknown-word guard both work as documented for ordinary words. Out-of-range jumps are rejected, never clamped.

---

**TL;DR (plain English):** I re-ran the other tester's experiments from scratch with my own recordings and six different computer voices, and the alarming parts are true. Someone saying "next flight", "obvious slide", "a round of applause" or "continue presenting" flips the presenter's slides — every single voice, every single time — and the software reports 100% certainty each time. I also proved end-to-end that an audience member saying "continue presenting" un-pauses the deck during the Q&A the pause exists to protect. Meanwhile the presenter saying "next slide please" or "okay next slide" does nothing at all, 6 times out of 6, because those polite words aren't in the tiny list of words the listener is allowed to know. The one-word fix the team's own notes describe is missing, and the code that builds the word list would silently delete it if anyone added it — I watched it happen and captured the warning. Two things I'd push back on: the claim that random conference chatter can jump you to a wrong slide didn't happen once in nearly 18 hours of my testing (it needs a specific "slide"-sounding word, which is a narrower and more predictable problem than described), and the "the team already knew and shipped it anyway" line isn't quite fair — that warning lives in a design document explicitly marked "not implemented, deferred" for a different piece of the code that was never built. Plain "next slide" and "go to slide twelve" work perfectly, and silence and background noise are completely safe.
